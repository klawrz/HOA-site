# HOPE Proposed Pricing Policy

Property platform and implementation pricing for condominium communities.

**Document history**
- Rev B — 2026-09-12. Separated "platform use" from "Slack support" everywhere the two were blurred together (see *Why this revision* below). Replaced the fixed "two designated Board contacts" with "the Board's designated officers," since a fixed headcount undercounts boards with three or more officers (Sampaguita has three: President, Treasurer, Secretary).
- Rev A — undated draft, originally circulated as `HOPE_Proposed_Pricing_Policy_Printable.pdf` (kept privately under `Discussion docs on real emails/`, not tracked here). Superseded by this file, which lives in the repo so changes are tracked in git going forward.

**Why this revision.** The original draft's customer-facing summary read: *"Normal use and Slack support for designated management and Board contacts are included."* That sentence attaches "normal use" to "designated contacts," which reads as a headcount cap on **using the platform itself** — not the intent. The platform is licensed per condominium, for every role, without limit; only the live Slack human-support channel is reasonably reserved for a small number of named people. This revision keeps those two facts in separate sentences everywhere, and generalizes the support headcount to "the Board's officers" rather than a fixed number that won't fit every board.

## Recommendation

HOPE should charge a predictable annual platform fee based on property capacity, together with a one-time implementation fee. The price does not vary with questions, conversations, transactions or system usage. This makes the annual cost known in advance and encourages owners, boards and property managers to use the platform fully.

## Pricing principles

- The condominium purchases shared infrastructure, not individual answers or transactions.
- The annual price combines a base platform fee with a unit-capacity component.
- Implementation is charged separately because document collection, configuration, data organization and training require significant initial work.
- **Platform use has no headcount limit** — every Board member, the property manager, and every owner gets full access at the one price, regardless of how many people that is.
- **Slack support** (a direct line to a human) is a separate, smaller benefit: it's included for the property manager and the Board's designated officers (typically the President, Treasurer and Secretary — however many that is for the property in question).
- Payment is tied to objective contractual milestones, not subjective satisfaction or particular business outcomes.

## Annual platform price

**Internal pricing formula:** US$2,400 annual base platform fee plus US$45 per unit per year, subject to a US$3,000 annual minimum. Quotations are rounded to the nearest US$100.

| Property band | Annual platform price | Implementation fee | Slack support included |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 30 units | US$3,000 to US$3,700 | US$2,000 | PM + the Board's officers |
| 32 to 100 units | US$3,800 to US$6,900 | US$5,000 | PM + the Board's officers |
| 101 to 300 units | US$6,900 to US$15,900 | US$10,000 | PM + the Board's officers |

**Band note.** Properties with 30 or 31 units use the same formula and would normally follow the middle-band implementation scope.

> **Open question — implementation-fee cliffs (not resolved in this revision).** A 100-unit property pays a US$5,000 implementation fee; a 101-unit property pays US$10,000 — double, for one more unit. The same jump happens at 29→30 units. The band note above doesn't actually remove this discontinuity, it just moves it from the annual price into the implementation fee. Worth smoothing (e.g. a per-unit implementation component, or band boundaries that don't sit one unit apart from a round number) before this is shown to a sophisticated buyer or a property manager comparing notes across clients. Left as-is here since it's a pricing-strategy decision, not a wording fix.

## What the platform fee includes

- HOPE access for the condominium, including Board, property-management and owner roles — **no limit on how many people, or how much they use it**.
- Ask HOPE and full use by every authorized user, at any volume.
- Document organization and institutional knowledge.
- Standard governance, financial and property-management reporting.
- Routine software updates, security maintenance and normal data storage.
- Slack support for the property manager and the Board's designated officers — a direct line to a human, distinct from platform use above.

## First and second year examples

Year 1 includes the one-time implementation fee plus the annual platform fee. Year 2 contains only the annual platform fee, assuming the property remains within the same capacity and scope.

| Example | Units | Annual platform | Setup fee | Year 1 total | Year 2 total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small founding case | 14 | US$3,000 | US$0 | US$3,000 | US$3,000 |
| Small standard case | 29 | US$3,700 | US$2,000 | US$5,700 | US$3,700 |
| Community lower case | 32 | US$3,800 | US$5,000 | US$8,800 | US$3,800 |
| Community reference case | 100 | US$6,900 | US$5,000 | US$11,900 | US$6,900 |
| Large lower case | 101 | US$6,900 | US$10,000 | US$16,900 | US$6,900 |
| Large reference case | 300 | US$15,900 | US$10,000 | US$25,900 | US$15,900 |

**Sampaguita founding treatment.** Sampaguita pays no implementation fee because it is the founding implementation and contributes to the creation of HOPE's repeatable onboarding process. Its illustrative first- and second-year platform cost is US$3,000 per year — with full platform use for its Board (three officers: President, Treasurer, Secretary), its property manager, and every owner, at no additional cost regardless of how much the platform is used.

## Implementation payment policy

| Milestone | Payment | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement signed | 30% | Due when onboarding is authorized and work begins |
| 90-day milestone | 70% | Due when the agreed implementation deliverables are completed |

**Acceptance is objective.** The balance is not conditional on subjective satisfaction, adoption levels or a particular business outcome. The implementation schedule and payment date may be adjusted where HOPE materially fails to deliver or where the client delays access, records or required decisions, according to the agreement.

**Implementation deliverables**
- Property structure, roles and permissions configured.
- Agreed documents collected, organized and imported.
- Core access and Ask HOPE operating on approved information, with designated contacts trained.

## Partner property manager model

**Recurring revenue share.** A participating property-management partner may receive 30% of annual platform revenue when the partner introduces the client and remains responsible for the ongoing local client relationship. HOPE retains 70% for platform operation, product development and technical support.

| Representative property | Annual revenue | Partner share (30%) | HOPE share (70%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 units | US$3,000 | US$900 | US$2,100 |
| 100 units | US$6,900 | US$2,070 | US$4,830 |
| 300 units | US$15,900 | US$4,770 | US$11,130 |

> **Open question — is 30% flat enough of a channel incentive? (not resolved in this revision).** At $900/year on a 14-unit property, the incentive to actively champion HOPE across a PM's whole portfolio is thin at the single-property level; it only becomes meaningful in aggregate across many clients. If Property Managers become the primary go-to-market channel (rather than pitching each HOA Board directly — see the reasoning that HOPE is easier to sell through one PM relationship than one board's internal politics at a time), this probably deserves a second look: e.g. a portfolio-scale bonus for a PM who brings multiple properties, not just a flat per-property percentage. Left as-is here since it's a strategy decision, not a wording fix.

**Implementation work is compensated separately.** The recurring 30% share should not be assumed to cover substantial data gathering, document cleanup, configuration or training. The implementation agreement should identify which party performs each task and how the implementation fee is divided.

## Commercial safeguards

- Annual fees are quoted and fixed in advance for the subscription term.
- There are no charges per question, conversation, transaction, report or owner interaction — and no charge for how many people use the platform.
- Slack support (the human support channel) is included for the property manager and the Board's designated officers; custom development and exceptional migration work are quoted separately.
- The condominium owns its information and can obtain an export on termination.
- Annual pricing may be reviewed at renewal for inflation, material scope changes or movement into another capacity band.
- The model should be reviewed after the first three external implementations using actual onboarding and support time.

## Proposed customer-facing summary

HOPE provides a predictable annual platform price based on the size of the condominium, with no additional costs of any kind: no per-question, per-report, per-transaction, or per-user charges. Every Board member, the property manager, and every owner gets full platform use at that one price. Implementation is charged once, with 30% payable when work begins and 70% at the defined 90-day delivery milestone. A direct Slack support line is also included for the property manager and the Board's officers.
