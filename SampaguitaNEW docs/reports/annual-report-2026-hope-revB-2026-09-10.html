The cost of running Sampaguita is rising, and the additional cost to owners in 2027 is material — on the order of USD 10,000–12,000 per villa of new cost versus the prior budget year. These items need owner and Board action before the Friday November 6, 2026 AGM.

  1. Operating budget up about +33% year on year.

    FY2027 proposed at USD 273,072 (MXN 4,642,223) vs a FY2026 budget of approximately USD 205,000 — up roughly USD 68,000. Driven by utilities being brought current after deferred 2025 invoices, the full landscaping contract, payroll taxes and IMSS for all three employees, and the beach concession now funded — and, in dollar terms, by the stronger peso. Detail in §3. 2025 recorded spending was about USD 150,000, so the cash increase owners feel is steeper still. The Board asks owners to review the budget for savings before voting (§3, Appendix A).

  2. Dues rise for every villa — permanently.

    Dues are each villa's allocation % of the operating budget, so the increase flows straight through. A 6.81% villa moves from about USD 14,000 to USD 18,596 a year (+~USD 4,600); Villa 14 (9.25%) about +USD 6,300. This is a permanent step-up, not a one-off. Per villa in §5.

  3. Roof: three villas have reported problems — a motion, not a fixed assessment.

    Three villas have reported roof water issues (Villa 7 has a logged inspection; the others are in current maintenance tickets). There is no property-wide roof condition report, and nothing in the records supports replacing all 14 roofs. The AGM will consider a motion to (a) assess the scope of the affected roofs and (b) approve remedial action funded by a special assessment — with the current tickets taken as sufficient basis to include the assessment in the motion. The reserve policy's provisional figure is USD 150,000 across 2027–2028 (~USD 5,515 per villa per year if split evenly 14 ways); the real amount and split follow the scope assessment.

  4. Reserve fund is only 37% funded.

    USD 37,313 as of September 2026 — 37% of the USD 100,000 target, USD 7,313 above the 30%-of-target floor. The gap to target is USD 62,687; the draft policy's 5-year plan implies a top-up of about USD 12,500/yr, while the FY2027 budget carries USD 10,294 — a figure for owners to settle at the AGM (§6).

What this means for owners

A permanently higher dues bill (~+USD 4,600/yr for a 6.81% villa, up to ~+USD 6,300 for Villa 14) plus a roof special assessment (amount to be set from the scope assessment; provisionally ~USD 5,515 per villa in 2027). Unconfirmed; for planning until the Board adopts the budget and owners approve the roof motion.

The year in brief — the Property Manager's clean-up

Sara Gabriela Alfaro Chavero took over property management on May 1, 2026, replacing the prior arrangement run through Associa. The handover exposed a real backlog, and the new PM has been working through it since. What has been found and fixed:

  • Utilities brought current. Electricity, water and propane invoices held back through 2025 for cash reasons were identified and paid off in early 2026. This is the single biggest reason the FY2027 utilities lines step up — they now reflect the true cost of the service, not a suppressed figure.
  • Beach concession funded. The annual federal maritime-zone concession payment (≈ USD 5,000/yr) had not been budgeted or paid; it is now on the budget and being kept current, protecting the concession title.
  • Payroll and IMSS corrected. Payroll taxes and social security had been budgeted for one employee; the PM re-sized them for all three staff, bringing the Association into line with its obligations as an employer.
  • Water metering restarted. Monthly per-villa water-meter readings are being taken again, with quarterly billing to owners to follow — so each villa pays for what it actually uses.
  • Filing register being rebuilt. No consolidated list of the Association's SAT / IMSS / INFONAVIT / state / municipal filings was maintained under the prior arrangement. The PM and Treasurer are reconstructing it (§7) so nothing lapses again.
  • Records tidied. The owner register, employee roster and vendor contracts are being reconciled against source documents.
For owners

Much of the FY2027 increase is the cost of catching up — paying for services that were quietly deferred rather than a sudden rise in the underlying cost of running the property. The new PM's work over the past few months is why the Association now has an accurate picture to budget from.
