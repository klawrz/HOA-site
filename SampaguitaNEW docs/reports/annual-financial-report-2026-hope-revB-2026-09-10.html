Financial alerts

  1. Operating budget up about +33% on FY2026.

    FY2027 proposed at USD 273,072 vs a FY2026 budget of approximately USD 205,000 — up roughly USD 68,000. About two-thirds is real (utilities catch-up, full landscaping, payroll/IMSS for three staff, beach concession); the rest is the stronger peso lifting the dollar figure. Owners are asked to review the budget for savings before adopting it (§3, Appendix A); a target near USD 250,000 has been raised.

  2. Dues rise for every villa.

    Dues are each villa's allocation % of the operating budget. A 6.81% villa moves to USD 18,596/yr (about +USD 4,600 on the FY2026 basis); Villa 14 to USD 25,259 (about +USD 6,300). Per villa in §4.

  3. Roof special assessment — amount not yet fixed.

    Three villas have reported roof water problems; there is no property-wide roof inspection. The AGM motion (§10 M6) authorises a scope assessment, the remedial work, and a special assessment to fund it, with current maintenance tickets accepted as sufficient basis. The reserve policy's provisional figure is USD 150,000 across 2027–2028 (~USD 5,515 per villa per year if split evenly).

  4. Reserve fund is only 37% funded.

    USD 37,313 against a USD 100,000 target — USD 7,313 above the 30%-of-target floor, USD 62,687 short of target. The draft policy's 5-year plan implies a top-up of about USD 12,500/yr; the FY2027 budget carries USD 10,294 (§8).

  5. 2026 operating actuals are not yet available.

    Owners cannot yet see 2026 spend vs the 2026 budget. The Treasurer's 2026 report and the year-to-date figures (§7) are being compiled and must be circulated before the meeting for Motion M1 to be taken.

  6. Property insurance lapses December 30, 2026.

    The contract on file ends then; renewal is not confirmed in the records (§10 M8).

  7. Restricted and operating cash are mixed.

    The Scotiabank USD account (USD 46,597) holds special-assessment money and Q4 2026 dues together. The two should be separated in the accounts so restricted funds are not spent on operations (§9).

  8. Keeping the grounds green is close to half the budget.

    Grounds & Landscaping (USD 46,020) plus Payroll (USD 35,059 — the grounds and maintenance crew's wages and statutory costs) come to USD 81,079, about 30% of the operating budget. Add the Water line (USD 39,118, most of it irrigation) and the three together are USD 120,197 — about 44% of the budget. This is the clearest single area to look at for savings: irrigation efficiency, the landscaping-contract scope, and crew size.
